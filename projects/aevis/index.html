The user decision

A brand or content team needs to understand what was observed, how that observation was produced and whether a suggested change is justified. The interface’s job is to preserve that context while making the next action clear.

A COMPLETE TASK

Follow the decision, including recovery.

  1. Confirm the context

    A preset updates brand, industry, competitors and query together. Custom edits are reviewed before an audit.

  2. Interpret a fixture

    A sample result shows its query, numerator, denominator, fixture date and provider/model status.

  3. Handle incomplete evidence

    Switch to no data, partial provider failure, contradictory responses or stale evidence; missing data never becomes a zero score.

  4. Review and roll back

    Inspect original and proposed content, approve a page-local draft, compare the revision and restore the previous draft. No website publication occurs.

Alternative considered and selection criteria
Alternative
A single confident score and automatic publication
Chosen direction
Explain each sample observation, uncertainty and review boundary
Reason and cost
Review is slower but makes no-data, conflicting answers and unexecuted changes understandable.

DECISIONS AND TRADEOFFS

Why this behavior earns its place.

Name the origin of a result

A simulated response can illustrate the interface but cannot substantiate a live cross-engine citation claim.

Tradeoff. Every displayed result needs its context, sample nature and collection limits visible beside the interpretation.

Keep audit context consistent

A selected preset must update brand, competitors, industry and the suggested question together.

Tradeoff. A user can customize the fields, so the final query context should be checked before execution.

Suggest, review and compare

Generated content is a proposal. A later comparison cannot promise a guaranteed rank or recommendation.

Tradeoff. Provider variability and missing results need to remain visible; publication is a separate deliberate action.
