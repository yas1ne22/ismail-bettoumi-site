Explore places. Shape a day.

A local Rome planner, starting with the map and an equivalent searchable place list.

  1. Explore a place from the map or list and add two stops to your day.
  2. Review the order, planned times, walking buffers and unresolved ticket checks.
  3. Move a stop, remove it and undo the removal. The other stop stays in your plan.

Research plan, criteria & accessibility checks

Recruit 6–8 recent trip planners, including group organizers and travelers with access needs. Observe their current tools before testing the journey.

Study taskObserveAcceptance target
Find three places and add them to a daySearch behavior, lost context, correct day selectionAt least 6 of 8 complete without facilitator rescue.
Explain the day’s routeMap/list correspondence and timing comprehensionAt least 6 of 8 identify stop order and one travel buffer.
Replace a stop, then undo itAwareness of changes and retained stopsAt least 6 of 8 restore the original plan unaided.
Identify what is not bookedDistinction between saved, planned and confirmedAll participants identify an unresolved ticket requirement.

Report qualitative counts, not population-wide success rates. Check keyboard navigation, a list alternative to the map, text scaling, empty states and clear booking status.

A COMPLETE TASK

Follow the decision, including recovery.

  1. Explore and add

    Find a place through the searchable list, inspect it and add it to the day.

  2. Read the shared plan

    Map numbering and the ordered list describe the same stops. Visit estimates are editable.

  3. Resolve a conflict

    Shorten a visit or remove a stop when the day exceeds the chosen end time. Ticket checks remain unresolved commitments.

  4. Change and undo

    Use buttons to reorder, replace a weather-sensitive stop or remove one, then restore the prior plan.

DECISIONS AND TRADEOFFS

Why this behavior earns its place.

Places before configuration

The original flow asked for preferences before giving a sense of the city. The revised study lets people inspect places and build a day while constraints remain visible.

Tradeoff. A visual map needs an equivalent list and keyboard controls; a place’s practical conditions must not disappear behind photography.

Reversible changes

Deleting or moving a stop should not silently disturb the rest of the day. Explicit order controls and undo make the effect inspectable.

Tradeoff. A route preview is a planning aid, not verified walking directions or a provider reservation.

Show unresolved constraints

Estimated duration, ticket checks and a chosen day end make overfull plans visible. A saved gallery stop is never presented as a confirmed ticket.

Tradeoff. Current sample data is illustrative. Opening hours, access and live availability still require a service integration.

Reflection / What the iteration changed

Make time as editable as the route.

The key shift: help travelers imagine the day before asking them to organize it.

Keep

Keep map pins and the place list connected to the same day. Explicit ordering and undo make a change visible without silently replacing the other stops.

Change next

Let the traveler adjust visit estimates when the day is overfull. The current planner updates timing from those edits, retains the last valid calculation after an invalid value, and labels ticket checks separately.

Question to test

Does exploring places first help people build and revise a workable day compared with the earlier configure-first flow? The two versions are available for a real comparative task study.
